# Creator-fee escrow retirement and deployment-deposit recovery

Status: design and read-only inspection, not an implemented migration. Internal engineering review, not an independent audit. No closure, mainnet deployment or funds transfer is performed by the tools described here.

## Recovery is conditional

The dedicated deployment wallet should be the upgrade authority and the reviewed refund destination. Eligible executable storage rent can be reclaimed only by retiring and closing the program. Network fees are spent. Buffer rent and user-funded escrow/claim account rent are distinct deposits; they must not be described as one deployer refund. Do not remove upgrade authority if eventual closure is required. The Solana loader authority can close directly: our app cannot enforce its safety checks against that authority. [Solana deployment and closure documentation](https://solana.com/docs/programs/deploying)

Closing while old allocation PDAs retain any DBC creator authority, DAMM position NFT, unclaimed balance or pending entitlement strands rights. A zero vault balance is insufficient, because trading creates future fees. X recipients who have never used OneOnly retain their shares. Changing the destination to an ordinary wallet transfers custody and does not preserve enforced splits or X verification.

## Supported route for moving future fees

Meteora's DBC source permits creator-authority transfer before the curve completes and after pool creation; intermediate migration states reject it. Additional post-migration lock checks apply to legacy DAMM v1. Thus completed DAMM v2 graduation does not itself prevent DBC creator transfer. Our allocation PDA must sign the CPI, so this must happen before closing the old executable. Transferring that authority does not move an already-created DAMM NFT. These observations are from upstream source and still require a test against the pinned deployed programs. [DBC transfer implementation](https://github.com/MeteoraAg/dynamic-bonding-curve/blob/main/programs/dynamic-bonding-curve/src/instructions/creator/ix_transfer_pool_creator.rs)

For DAMM v2, ownership is checked through the position NFT token account. Meteora's Dynamic Fee Sharing (DFS) SDK documents changing that account's owner to the fee-vault PDA, then collecting position fees through the vault. This changes fee control without withdrawing locked liquidity; every actual locked/unlocked position and token-account extension must be rehearsed. [DAMM position checks](https://github.com/MeteoraAg/docs/blob/main/developer-guides/damm-v2/program/instructions.mdx), [DFS SDK](https://github.com/MeteoraAg/dynamic-fee-sharing-sdk/blob/main/docs.md)

An existing Meteora DFS vault can avoid deploying another bespoke executable, but it is not a universal replacement:

- The inspected implementation accepts 2–5 wallet entries, whereas OneOnly allows 1–8 X recipients. Do not add fake recipients or silently truncate shares. [Initialization validation](https://github.com/MeteoraAg/dynamic-fee-sharing/blob/main/programs/dynamic-fee-sharing/src/instructions/ix_initialize_fee_vault.rs)
- Collection through DFS requires a shareholder signer, and withdrawal requires the recipient signer. It does not retain OneOnly's X-post gate or permit arbitrary keeper collection. Every recipient must bind and explicitly consent to wallet-only future claims. [Collection authorization](https://github.com/MeteoraAg/dynamic-fee-sharing/blob/main/programs/dynamic-fee-sharing/src/instructions/ix_fund_by_claiming_fee.rs), [Claim authorization](https://github.com/MeteoraAg/dynamic-fee-sharing/blob/main/programs/dynamic-fee-sharing/src/instructions/ix_claim_fee.rs)
- The inspected two-token collection whitelist protects only the quote/token-B destination. Other-token destinations are intentionally not validated. Restrict a direct successor to provably quote-only fees both before and after graduation; resolve rewards and other fee rights separately. Two-mint allocations cannot safely be generalized onto this design. [Action constraints](https://github.com/MeteoraAg/dynamic-fee-sharing/blob/main/programs/dynamic-fee-sharing/src/constants.rs)
- DFS uses fixed-point fee-per-share accounting. Exact configured weights can match, but rounding differs from OneOnly's cumulative floors. Disclose that change in consent and test small-unit funding/claims. Old partially paid claims cannot be preserved by simply funding a new common split vault. [DFS accounting](https://github.com/MeteoraAg/dynamic-fee-sharing/blob/main/programs/dynamic-fee-sharing/src/state/fee_vault.rs)

If every recipient becomes bound and consents, a compatible allocation can migrate to a verified DFS vault. Until then, leave that allocation operational. An unbound recipient's share cannot be assigned to the old PDA and then survive closing that PDA's executable. Allocations outside these constraints need a separately reviewed successor or continued service; no arbitrary administrator-wallet fallback exists.

## Required on-chain migration specification

This is a specification for new reviewed instructions, not implemented functionality:

1. Disable new allocations, while preserving existing collection and valid claims. Register a single migration manifest for each allocation. Include network, old/new program and vault, immutable shares, each on-chain identity-to-wallet binding and version, claim-policy change, deadlines and nonce. Require all beneficiary wallet signatures; a server assertion or administrator choice is insufficient consent.
2. Pin the accepted DFS executable and reviewed version. Decode the complete fee-vault account; check its owning program, discriminator, PDA seeds/bump, quote mint/token program, token-vault address/authority, zero funding/checkpoints, exact recipients and exact basis-point weights, unused entries and no extra recipients. Never trust the client planner's booleans. Reject unsupported mint extensions.
3. Finalize each allocation atomically if packet/CPI limits permit: collect final DBC and every relevant DAMM fee into the old accounting, calculate each unpaid floor(totalReceived × bps / 10,000) minus cumulative claimed, pay only the bound recipient's correct token account and update claimed totals, transfer DBC creator authority and all DAMM NFT ownership to the checked successor, and record completed migration. Base-asset obligations, remaining DBC fees after graduation and any rewards cannot be omitted. If multiple transactions are necessary, design a durable resumable state machine that cannot double-pay, lose interim trading revenue, or strand rights after partial completion.
4. Prevent a race with concurrent claim, collection, graduation and new position creation using writable allocation/config state and explicit stage checks. Reconcile each position, including donated positions. Do not rely solely on off-chain scans for an atomic completion proof.
5. Settle old entitlements individually. For example, if equal recipients earned 100 and the first already claimed 50, sending the remaining 50 to a new equal vault would give the first another 25 and short the second 25. Funding the successor with old leftovers is incorrect. Define a recipient-approved dust/donation disposition; those amounts are not administrator profit.
6. Preserve replay and binding continuity. Closing claim/receipt accounts early must never enable a previously authorized claim to pay again. Store per-allocation migration tombstones and an authoritative registration/completion count or registry; an empty filtered RPC response is not completion evidence.
7. Reconcile all migrated allocations, residual external rights, balances and account rent. Close only accounts whose deposits have a specified rightful recipient. A later loader closure is a separate user-reviewed transaction after migration receipts and successor claims are proven.

## Tools in this change

`packages/fee-escrow/src/retirement.ts` exports read-only tools. They are intentionally not wired to a destructive button and create no transaction instructions:

- `inspectRetirement` checks expected chain genesis, loader/program-data linkage, nominated authority/refund destination and finalized unfiltered escrow account inventory. It computes per-mint unpaid base-unit entitlements and counts unbound identities. RPC failures, malformed data, unknown/orphan accounting and wrong authorities become blockers. External token accounts, NFTs and uncollected Meteora fees are not included, so even an empty result cannot approve closure.
- `planDfsRetirement` tests the narrow 2–5 bound, consenting, quote-only successor candidate against exact shares and a fresh vault. Its supplied observations and consent list are not cryptographic evidence. It can mark a candidate suitable for devnet rehearsal, but never authorizes migration or closure.
- `inspectUnusedDeployment` identifies a narrower business-abort candidate: only valid config/control accounts, finalized pause, reviewed artifact matching deployed bytes with zero trailing padding, unchanged original deployment slot, matching upgrade authority and refund destination, and empty SPL/Token-2022 account scans for the program/config/control addresses. It requires the original deployment slot from an independently checked finalized receipt. Missing control, upgraded code, extra state, token accounts (even empty), stale scans or RPC failures block it. It returns a candidate for manual unused-program closure review, **not** permission to close.

All return `safeToClose: false`. For the unused-program path, independently confirm the original deployment receipt/artifact provenance and absence of pending upgrade/unpause/initialization operations, then prepare a separately reviewed closure/refund transaction. The caller-supplied slot is not proof, scans are not atomic and known-PDA token scans do not enumerate arbitrary external rights. No loader-close builder is included. A lifetime registration counter or immutable retirement seal would provide stronger evidence than current snapshots.

For active allocations, closure remains blocked until the migration protocol and its chain-verified completion proof exist. A temporary option is to stop new allocations and keep the existing program, verifier and claim interface running; this preserves claims but does not recover executable rent. The current emergency pause also pauses claims, so it is not by itself a permanent “no new launches, claims continue” retirement mode; that separation needs an explicit implementation.

## Devnet acceptance before enabling retirement

Use a disposable program/allocations; never close the active escrow as a shortcut. Cover two recipients with partial prior claims; an unbound recipient blocking retirement; all consent signatures; missing/wrong signature and substituted vault rejection; quote-only configuration validation; Token-2022 and SPL account handling; permanently locked and unlocked DAMM positions; pre-graduation transfer followed by graduation under the successor; post-graduation DBC remainder plus DAMM transfer; unexpected extra position; repeated/resumed migration; trade/claim races; rounding dust; successor shareholder collection and individual claim; complete account-deposit reconciliation; and final loader refund verification. Mainnet requires reviewed deployed-program compatibility, independent security review, final artifact/cost checks and the dedicated wallet's explicit signing approval.
